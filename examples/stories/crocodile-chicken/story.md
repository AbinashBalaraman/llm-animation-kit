# Story and directing plan

A protective hen mistakes a lonely crocodile for a threat, until his careful rescue of her egg gives her a reason to trust him.

## Story

Poppy has chosen a sunny patch beside the river for her nest. She stands close to her egg, pleased with her little corner of the world. Then Moss raises his broad snout from the water. He opens his mouth, revealing a row of enormous teeth. Poppy recoils and tucks her wing close. To her, those teeth mean danger. Moss closes his mouth and stays back.

A gust bends across the bank. The egg rocks, tips over the straw rim, and rolls downhill. Poppy rushes after it with an outstretched wing, but the egg crosses the bank before she can reach it. It is falling toward the river.

Moss sees the falling egg. He moves underneath it and lifts his snout, keeping his mouth closed. The egg lands on top. He dips slightly to cushion it, then steadies himself. The egg is intact. For a moment he holds still, giving Poppy time to see what has happened.

In a closer view, Poppy's raised wing lowers and her tense head relaxes. Moss comes to the bank and carefully lowers the egg into its nest. He then withdraws, leaving the choice to approach with Poppy.

Poppy notices the flower beside her. She plucks it and makes a joyful hop over the safe nest toward Moss. This time she chooses to get close to those teeth. She offers the flower; Moss accepts it and closes his eye softly. The egg remains safe behind them. They finish side by side: her fear has changed into trust because of something he actually did.

## Who, what, when, where, why and how

**Who:** Poppy is a protective hen guarding one egg. Moss is a gentle crocodile whose large teeth make him look dangerous. Their unequal size creates the initial misunderstanding.

**What:** Poppy wants to keep her egg safe. A gust rolls it toward the river. Moss catches it, brings it back intact, and receives a flower from Poppy as a voluntary gesture of trust.

**When:** One quiet morning, in a continuous encounter lasting about twenty-two seconds on screen. There is no time jump to a different day or unexplained new family.

**Where:** A grassy riverbank with a nest on the dry left side and water to the right. Wide shots establish that geography; the catch and the hen reaction receive closer views.

**Why:** Poppy recoils because she sees teeth beside her vulnerable egg. Moss helps because he is gentle, despite his appearance. Poppy changes her judgment after watching him return the egg instead of eating it.

**How:** A gust dislodges the egg; Poppy reaches but cannot catch it; Moss rises underneath and cushions it on his broad snout; he lowers it into the nest. Poppy relaxes, plucks a nearby flower, and hops over the nest to offer it to him.

## Shot script

### strangers — 4 seconds

**Framing:** Shared wide view of the bank, nest and two distinct silhouettes.

**Action:** The hen guards her egg; the croc opens and closes his jaw.

**Reaction:** She recoils and folds her wing.

**Continuity in:** Establish the hen left, river right, and the egg in the nest.

**Continuity out:** The egg remains in the nest; they are still apart.

### danger — 3 seconds

**Framing:** Medium-wide view keeping the nest, sloping bank and water together.

**Action:** Wind sweeps across the bank and the egg rolls toward water.

**Reaction:** The hen reaches and rushes after it without making contact.

**Continuity in:** Same bank and egg; a gust is visible before movement.

**Continuity out:** Cut while the egg is falling, retaining its approximate world position and rotation.

### rescue — 4 seconds

**Framing:** Close water-level view, with the snout and falling egg large in frame.

**Action:** Moss rises underneath; the egg switches to snout-relative movement at contact.

**Reaction:** He cushions the landing and holds still to show the egg is safe.

**Continuity in:** The egg continues falling; no pass through the body or unexplained reversal.

**Continuity out:** The intact egg is balanced on a closed snout, high above the water.

### relief — 2 seconds

**Framing:** Closer two-shot centered on the hen, including the saved egg at frame right.

**Action:** The hen looks at the intact egg that Moss is carrying toward her.

**Reaction:** Her wing lowers, head relaxes and eyes soften.

**Continuity in:** Egg remains on the snout; the hen still waits on dry land.

**Continuity out:** Her relief is established before the return and gift.

### return — 4 seconds

**Framing:** Medium two-shot with both the nest and snout contact visible.

**Action:** Moss lowers the egg into the nest, then withdraws to give her space.

**Reaction:** Poppy watches, lowers her wing, and stays near the flower.

**Continuity in:** Keep egg on the snout until the deposit; plant the flower beside her before pickup.

**Continuity out:** Egg is in the nest at the same position used in the next shot; Moss has backed away.

### friends — 5 seconds

**Framing:** Shared view showing the safe nest, approach arc, flower handoff and faces.

**Action:** Poppy lifts the flower, hops over the nest, and offers it at his snout.

**Reaction:** Moss accepts, closes his eye softly, and stays beside her.

**Continuity in:** The flower starts at its planted position; the egg and nest do not teleport.

**Continuity out:** Hold their chosen proximity and the safe egg; end text is optional support, not the reason for friendship.

## Writer's self-review

**Motivation: Why does the hen fear him, and what concrete evidence changes her mind?**

The opening teeth and recoil establish fear. His gentle catch, intact return and retreat supply evidence of kindness. Her later approach is voluntary.

Revision: Added an explicit relief close-up before the gift.

**Causality: Can a viewer follow the rescue without captions, and does each event cause the next?**

The gust precedes the rolling egg, the bank precedes the fall, his rise precedes contact, and the return precedes her gift. Each shot has a visible cause and changed condition.

Revision: Kept the egg falling in the same direction across the rescue cut and removed explanatory captions.

**Stakes: What could be lost, and why does this matter to these characters?**

The single egg is visibly guarded from the start. Losing it to the river matters to the protective hen. The crocodile risks continued rejection, not a world-ending disaster.

Revision: The nest and egg stay visible in the resolution.

**Clarity: Which exact images prove rescue, return and friendship rather than announcing them?**

Egg resting intact on the snout proves the catch; egg separated from him inside the nest proves return; her unforced approach and flower handoff express trust.

Revision: Added closer contact and reaction framing; removed scene-heading captions.

**Ending: Does the ending pay off the opening relationship through a choice?**

She first moves away from his teeth and finally moves toward him with a gift. He does not demand it. The ending resolves the same relationship that the opening established.

Revision: Hold the shared final pose after the handoff.

**Filmability: Can this shot script be staged with the actual 2D tools, and what must still be inspected?**

Yes: joint poses, a sampled arc, snout-anchor tracks and discrete matched handoffs. Exact contact, cut positions and readable reactions still require rendered inspection; this plan alone cannot prove them.

Revision: Use temporal strips around catch, egg deposit, flower pickup and handoff; disclose that normal-speed playback is unassessed.

These are authored production notes. Structural validation and self-review do not independently establish story or animation quality.
