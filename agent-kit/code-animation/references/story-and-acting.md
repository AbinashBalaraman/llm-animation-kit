# Turn a script into visible animation

Identify what changes on screen: subject, action, object, location and result. Preserve user-specified beats and dialogue; fill only gaps needing creative choices. Separate essential requirements from embellishment. A generic prompt may need a short script; an existing screenplay already supplies it.

For each shot decide what the viewer learns, what moves, where attention starts/ends, and the last readable pose. Write the contact or reveal frame before decorative motion. Travelling needs spatial change; winning needs evidence of victory; a chart needs the requested values. Captions support the event, not replace it.

Draw a visual identity before building every shot. For animals, consider species silhouette, ears, muzzle, limbs and tail. For objects, include construction and affordances. For people, choose proportions and clothing. Lock shapes and colours in reusable assets. Different poses may need new drawings; a single frontal rig is not compulsory.

Build gestures with attention/anticipation, main action, contact/result and settle. Place group origins at joints. Head, eyes, hands and root can lead or follow by small offsets. For walking, coordinate root travel, alternating legs and foot placement; avoid sliding an unchanged silhouette unless stylistically intentional.

Plan both sides of physical contact: hand path, prop location, and what changes after contact. A held prop can belong to a moving group, then switch to a separate placed version at release. Match world pose across the change. No solver guarantees contact: inspect that exact frame and its neighbors.

Comedy needs a legible expectation before the surprising change. Let motivation cause the payoff. Hold long enough to register it, then react or cut. Repeated waves/smiles do not create a comic event.

For educational/abstract scripts, build the objects and relationships being explained. Use transformations, highlighting and sequencing to communicate change. Keep data correct; do not force every topic into a cartoon plot.

If a request depends on unsupported 3D or advanced simulation, identify the exact requirement and needed conventional backend. Never silently substitute an unrelated 2D demo. The portable v2 backend is general authored vector 2D, not an unrestricted film engine.
