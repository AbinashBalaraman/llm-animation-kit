# Inspect the animation

Review at normal speed, then inspect critical frame ranges. Check whether the viewer can tell what attracted attention and identify the intended reaction without reading the brief. Look for a gaze lead, a readable pause, expression change and a clear gesture silhouette.

At cuts, compare identity, color, gaze and the action's progress. Camera changes should reveal the same underlying performance. Check ears, antenna and raised hands against the crop, plus the title area. The framing diagnostic is advisory: an intentional close-up may crop feet, but that needs conscious review.

Check the actual revised output against the requested edit. A timing revision should preserve colors, rig and unrelated action data. A camera revision should preserve world evaluation. A technically valid scene may still need pose, timing or composition improvements.

Known limitations: this front-view puppet has no grasp/contact solver, speech, arbitrary perspective or 3D collisions. A reach is expressive posing. Do not report a physical interaction that the tool never computed.

Review records state accepted/rejected, reviewer type and observations. Preserve unresolved issues. The worked examples are authored/inspected demonstrations; they do not measure model parity or professional animation quality.
